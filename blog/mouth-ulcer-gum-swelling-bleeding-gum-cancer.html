Does a Mouth Ulcer, Gum Swelling or Bleeding Gum Mean Cancer?

By Dr. Sudip Roy, B.D.S. – Sr. Consultant Cosmetic Dental Surgeon & Oral Implantologist
Founder: Dr. S. Roy’s SMILE Dental Clinic, Jalpaiguri.

⏱️ 4-minute read | Updated: August 2026

Most mouth ulcers, gum swelling, and bleeding gums are not cancer. The purpose of a dental examination is not to assume cancer — it is to identify the actual cause and decide whether further investigation is needed.

The most important point on this page: A normal platelet count does not rule out oral cancer, and a low platelet count does not mean cancer. Biopsy — not a blood test — is the only way to confirm oral cancer with certainty.

Common, Non-Cancerous Causes

  • Mouth ulcers: local irritation, minor trauma, stress; most heal in 7–14 days
  • Gum swelling: gingivitis, periodontitis, dental infection, local irritation
  • Bleeding gums: gingivitis and periodontal disease, most commonly — see our detailed causes guide

Usually Fine

Ulcer resolves within 2 weeks · swelling improves with cleaning/hygiene · bleeding tied to brushing habit · no lump, numbness, or voice change

Arrange an Examination If:

  • A mouth ulcer lasts more than 3 weeks
  • A persistent lump, or red/white patch
  • Pain that doesn't resolve, or a painless sore that won't heal
  • Numbness, difficulty swallowing, or a hoarse voice that persists
  • A lump in the neck, or unexplained loosening of a tooth

What a Dentist Checks — Before Any Biopsy

A clinical exam looks at the lips, gums, tongue, cheeks, palate and floor of the mouth; checks size, colour, and texture of any abnormal area; and reviews history — how long it's been present, whether it's changing, and relevant risk factors like tobacco or alcohol use. Not every lesion needs a biopsy — the decision depends on these findings.
